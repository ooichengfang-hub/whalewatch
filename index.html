About Whale Watch

What it shows. Exchange trades are anonymous, so nobody can see who a whale is. What you see here is every large taker order on Binance for your coins as it prints: an order that buys from (BUY, green) or sells into (SELL, red) the order book worth at least your threshold in USDT.

How orders are built. Binance aggTrades (public stream) already group fills of one taker at one price. A large market order that sweeps several price levels prints several aggTrades at almost the same moment, so consecutive aggTrades of the same coin, market and side that print within 100 ms of the first one (Settings) are merged into one whale order. Two different traders hitting the same side within 100 ms can be merged too: it is a best-effort reconstruction.

Whale flow sums whale buy $ and sell $ for 5 min, 15 min, 1 h and 24 h. The 24 h column only counts what this page has seen since it was opened.

Liquidations come from Binance's public futures liquidation stream (one per symbol per second at most, so it is a sample). BTC on-chain shows new unconfirmed Bitcoin transactions from mempool.space whose total outputs are at least the set amount; that total includes change returning to the sender, and the wallet owners are unknown (often exchanges moving their own coins).

Running 24/7. It only watches while this page is open and the computer is awake. On Windows set Settings → System → Power → Screen and sleep → "When plugged in, put my device to sleep after" = Never, keep the tab open (it may be in the background or minimized), and press Wake to stop the screen from sleeping where supported. Streams reconnect automatically with backoff, fall back to REST polling, and refill missed trades after a reconnect. For alerts while the PC is off you would need something running on a server.

Data: Binance USDT-M futures and spot public market data, mempool.space public API. No account, no API keys. Times are UTC+8 (Asia/Kuala_Lumpur). Not financial advice.
